Discovery

The opportunity

The idea originated from a broader organizational goal: building an AI product that could demonstrate the company's AI capability and establish a footprint in the growing AI market.

While exploring gaps in the Microsoft Dynamics 365 ecosystem, we identified an opportunity around recommendations and next-best-action guidance for sales representatives.

Customer conversations reinforced the opportunity. One customer had specifically enquired about an AI capability that could recommend actions to sales representatives to improve prospect conversion and recommend products for cross-sell and upsell scenarios.

Further discovery with sales representatives highlighted recurring challenges around manual prioritization, fragmented information and deciding what action to take next within CRM.

Problems identified

  • Manual prioritization of leads and opportunities.
  • Inconsistent follow-up on high-intent buyers.
  • Static workflows that do not adapt to changing customer behavior.
  • Limited contextual guidance within the CRM UI.
  • Lack of explainability can reduce trust in AI.

Product opportunity

  • Surface actionable guidance directly in CRM.
  • Use historical success patterns to inform actions.
  • Make recommendations explainable.
  • Provide configurable AI capabilities.
  • Help CRM become an active, AI-guided workspace.

Product Strategy

The key product decision

Early in the product definition, we considered two related capabilities: a Recommendation Engine and a Next Best Action Engine.

Strategic Trade-off

Recommendation Engine vs. Next Best Action

We decided to prioritize Next Best Action. Competitive analysis showed that recommendation capabilities were already becoming common, while Microsoft was also rolling out recommendation-oriented Copilot capabilities across its modules. Next Best Action represented a clearer gap and an opportunity to establish our AI footprint ahead of Microsoft's direction.

This decision allowed the team to focus the initial product around actionable, contextual guidance rather than attempting to build both capabilities at once.

Product Experience

Designing for actionable AI

A core product principle was to make AI guidance useful within the user's existing CRM workflow rather than requiring sales representatives to interpret separate reports or dashboards.

I contributed to defining the CRM workflow, the recommendation experience, the Next Best Action Timeline and the Next Best Action Digest. These workflows were reviewed and approved by management and the C-suite.

Reflection

What this experience taught me

Explainability and contextual guidance are important parts of making AI useful in an enterprise workflow.
