Case Study 03 · E-commerce Digital Transformation

Turning complex transformation requirements into build-ready product stories.

Contributing to Herbalife's global digital transformation by translating business requirements into clear, testable user stories and partnering with Product Owners and Engineering through delivery and functional validation.

E-commerce Digital Transformation Enterprise Agile Product Requirements
Company Herbalife International
Timeline Aug 2022 – Mar 2023
Role Functional Consultant
Focus Requirements → Delivery
01 · Context

Building the foundation for a global digital transformation

Herbalife was undertaking a major digital transformation initiative aimed at modernizing its member-facing technology and creating a more integrated digital experience for distributors and customers.

In August 2022, Herbalife announced Herbalife One, a multi-year, approximately $400M digital technology initiative. The program was designed around a modern and flexible architecture, deeper data analytics, improved performance and faster deployment of future capabilities.

The transformation included capabilities spanning onboarding, training, wellness content, shopping tools, community and support. This made requirements clarity particularly important across a complex global product environment.

02 · Product Challenge

How do you turn business intent into something engineering can build?

The challenge was not simply creating new digital functionality. The transformation required high-level business requirements to be converted into clear, actionable and testable product requirements.

How do we translate complex business requirements into clear, testable functionality without losing the original business intent?

  • Multiple Product Owner and business stakeholders
  • Cross-functional engineering teams
  • Complex functional dependencies
  • Evolving transformation requirements
  • Global digital and commerce workflows

04 · Discovery & Requirement Understanding

Understanding what the business actually needed

My first step was to discuss requirements with Product Owners and understand the underlying business need rather than simply documenting the initial request.

STEP 01

Understand

Discuss the requirement with the PO team and understand the expected business outcome.

STEP 02

Clarify

Identify ambiguity, missing scenarios, business rules and functional dependencies.

STEP 03

Refine

Convert the requirement into clear, actionable product behavior.

05 · User Stories

Turning requirements into build-ready stories

Once requirements were sufficiently refined, I translated them into structured user stories in Azure DevOps.

What the story needed to clarify

  • Who is performing the action?
  • What does the user need to accomplish?
  • What business outcome is expected?
  • What rules govern the behavior?
  • What happens in alternate scenarios?

What I documented

  • User story
  • Functional requirements
  • Acceptance criteria
  • Business rules
  • Relevant edge cases and assumptions
06 · Acceptance Criteria

Making requirements testable

A key part of my contribution was turning ambiguous expectations into observable product behavior.

Requirement → Expected behavior → Acceptance criteria → Test scenario

For example, instead of simply stating that a user should be able to complete an action, the requirement could be broken down into specific conditions, expected system behavior and outcomes.

This created a shared definition of "done" for Product Owners, Engineering and validation.

07 · Stakeholder Alignment

Aligning Product Owners before development

After refining the requirements and preparing the user story, I worked with the Product Owner team to review and obtain sign-off.

This helped surface ambiguity before development rather than discovering it after implementation.

08 · Engineering Collaboration

Keeping business intent intact during development

During development, I worked with the development team to clarify functional questions related to the requirements.

Questions could involve business rules, expected behavior, edge cases or interpretation of the user story.

Don't answer from assumption. Go back to the requirement and the underlying business intent.

This helped maintain alignment between what the business intended and what engineering implemented.

09 · Functional Validation

Does the delivered functionality behave as intended?

Once development was completed, I validated the functionality against the agreed requirements and acceptance criteria.

01

Review

Review the user story and acceptance criteria.

02

Test

Execute expected and alternate functional scenarios.

03

Validate

Compare actual behavior against the agreed requirements.

Validation included

  • Happy paths
  • Alternate scenarios
  • Business rules
  • Edge cases
  • Acceptance criteria
  • Expected system behavior

11 · Key Challenge

Bridging business language and technical implementation

Business stakeholders naturally communicate in terms of desired outcomes, while Engineering needs precise functional behavior.

Business language

"The customer should have an easy ordering experience."

Product language

What action should the user take? What conditions apply? What happens next? What are the exceptions? What does success look like?

My role was to convert business intent → product behavior → testable acceptance criteria.

12 · Global Product Context

Designing for a global digital ecosystem

Herbalife One was designed as a global platform serving distributors and customers across multiple markets.

Herbalife later described a phased rollout strategy, beginning with the Singapore website launch before expanding the modernized experience to additional markets. This illustrates the complexity of building consistent global capabilities while accounting for market-specific needs.

Global product thinking requires both consistency in the platform and flexibility in the experience.

13 · Outcome

Improving the connection between requirements and delivery

My contribution was focused on delivery quality rather than ownership of the overall Herbalife One business outcome.

  • Translated business requirements into structured user stories.
  • Defined clearer acceptance criteria and expected behavior.
  • Aligned Product Owners and Engineering before development.
  • Resolved functional ambiguity during development.
  • Validated completed functionality against requirements.
  • Identified gaps and supported refinement before completion.

The broader Herbalife One initiative subsequently progressed into modernized Herbalife.com experiences and distributor e-commerce capabilities across additional markets.

These later program milestones are included as context and are not presented as personal achievements from my Aug 2022–Mar 2023 engagement.

14 · Product Learnings

What this experience taught me about product development

01 · Clarity is a product skill

Well-defined requirements can prevent significant downstream ambiguity and rework.

02 · Bridge business and technology

Strong product development requires understanding both business intent and technical implementation.

03 · Make "done" measurable

Acceptance criteria turn subjective expectations into observable product behavior.

04 · Validation is part of ownership

Shipping is not the end. The delivered functionality must still match the intended user and business outcome.
