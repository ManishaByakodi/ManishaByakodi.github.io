Context

Supporting a global digital transformation in a complex e-commerce ecosystem

Herbalife International is a global company operating through a multi-level marketing model and selling nutrition and dietary supplement products.

During my involvement from August 2022 to March 2023, the organization was undertaking a broader digital transformation aimed at modernizing its technology ecosystem and improving digital experiences for its members and customers.

Within this environment, product and technology teams needed to translate business requirements into functionality that could be developed consistently across a complex digital ecosystem.

My Contribution

I focused on making business requirements clear enough to build and test

As a Functional Consultant, my responsibility was to work closely with Product Owners to understand requirements, refine them into actionable user stories, obtain sign-off, support Engineering with functional clarification and validate the delivered functionality.

Discovery

The first challenge was turning business conversations into precise requirements

Business requirements often begin as high-level outcomes or conversations. Before development could begin, those requirements needed to be clarified, structured and made actionable.

Product Owner Discussions

Discussed requirements with Product Owners to understand the intended business outcome, workflow and expected functionality.

Requirement Refinement

Broke down high-level requirements into smaller functional requirements that could be translated into development stories.

Gap Identification

Asked clarifying questions and identified ambiguities before stories were handed over for development.

Product Definition

Making every story clear, actionable and testable

Once the requirement was understood, I translated the functional intent into user stories in Azure DevOps. The objective was to ensure that the development team had enough clarity to understand what needed to be built and that the resulting functionality could be validated against defined expectations.

This required balancing business language with the level of functional detail needed by Engineering and QA.

Product Thinking

The key challenge: bridging business language and technical implementation

Business intent vs. implementation detail

A good requirement had to work for both sides

Product Owners needed requirements that reflected the intended business outcome, while developers needed enough functional clarity to implement the expected behavior. My role was to bridge that gap without unnecessarily prescribing the technical implementation.

Business perspective

  • What problem is the functionality solving?
  • What should the user be able to accomplish?
  • What business behavior is expected?
  • What does the Product Owner need to approve?

Engineering perspective

  • What functionality needs to be implemented?
  • What should happen in different scenarios?
  • What conditions should be satisfied?
  • How can the functionality be validated?

Validation

Closing the loop from requirement to working functionality

My involvement did not stop once the user story was handed over to Engineering. I also validated the developed functionality against the requirements and expected behavior.

This allowed functional gaps or deviations to be identified before the functionality was considered complete.

Review

Reviewed the developed module or user story against the documented functional requirements.

Functional Testing

Validated the functionality and expected user behavior across relevant scenarios.

Feedback

Identified functional issues and clarified expected behavior with the relevant teams.

Delivery Principle

A story was not complete simply because it was developed

The final check was whether the delivered functionality matched the agreed requirement and acceptance criteria. This helped connect requirements, implementation and functional validation into one delivery loop.

Reflection

What this experience taught me

Good product requirements start with understanding the "why".

Writing a user story effectively requires understanding the underlying business objective rather than simply documenting what someone initially asks for.

Clarity is a product delivery skill.

The ability to convert ambiguous business discussions into precise, testable requirements can significantly improve alignment across Product, Engineering and QA.

Delivery is more than writing requirements.

Staying involved through Engineering clarification and functional validation provides important context on whether the intended product behavior was actually delivered.
