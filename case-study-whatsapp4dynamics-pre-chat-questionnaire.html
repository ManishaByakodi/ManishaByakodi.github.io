Automating Pre-Chat Information Collection

01 · THE PROBLEM

The initial request wasn't the real problem

The feature originated from an existing WhatsApp4Dynamics customer's request to integrate survey responses within Microsoft Dynamics 365 CRM.

Through further discussions and customer interviews, I discovered that the underlying need was broader: organizations wanted to automate the collection of prerequisite information from WhatsApp users before an agent responded.

Many WhatsApp conversations started with a short, generic message such as "Hi." Sales agents then had to manually ask questions such as the prospect's name, product of interest, or reason for contacting the business.

Problem we reframed: How can we automatically collect the information an organization needs before an agent begins handling a WhatsApp conversation?

02 · DISCOVERY

Understanding the opportunity

I conducted customer interviews to understand the information agents repeatedly needed, where manual questioning created friction, and whether the problem was specific to one use case or applicable across different business workflows.

I also conducted direct and indirect competitor research. Existing Dynamics 365 competitors offered similar questionnaire functionality, but I identified usability limitations in their implementations.

I additionally researched third-party applications to explore alternative approaches and gather ideas for a more flexible configuration experience.

Opportunity identified: Turn a customer-specific request into a reusable, configurable product capability.
03 · THE SOLUTION

A configurable Pre-Chat Questionnaire

I defined a configurable Pre-Chat Questionnaire that allowed WhatsApp4Dynamics customers to collect the information they needed before agent intervention.

Instead of prescribing a fixed questionnaire, the configuration was designed to support different customer requirements across Sales, Customer Service, and Marketing.

04 · PRODUCT DECISION

Simple vs. advanced configuration

One of the key product decisions was determining how much configuration flexibility to include in the initial release.

Advanced configuration

Greater flexibility, but increased complexity, development effort, and release time.

Simple configuration · Selected

Faster time to market, lower initial complexity, and an opportunity to validate customer adoption before adding advanced capabilities.

Decision: Launch the simplest useful version, validate adoption, gather customer feedback, and use evidence to determine where additional configuration was warranted.

06 · RESULTS

Adoption validated the core solution

The feature automated the collection of prerequisite information before agent intervention, reducing repetitive questioning and giving agents more context at the start of a conversation.

The strongest measurable signal was customer adoption. Adoption was tracked through post-launch customer interactions, including requests for feature configuration guidance.

~90% of new customers adopted the feature, while ~50% of existing customers implemented it after launch.

The feature helped organizations automate repetitive information collection, support response-time SLAs, improve the WhatsApp customer experience, and create more efficient sales and customer-service workflows.

These adoption signals also provided evidence that the simple MVP addressed a meaningful customer need and created a basis for considering more advanced configuration capabilities.

07 · LEARNINGS & NEXT STEPS

What I learned as a PM

Start with the problem

The stated customer request may describe a solution, not the underlying problem. Customer discovery helped uncover the broader workflow issue.

Don't overbuild the MVP

A focused MVP allowed us to validate the core use case before investing in more complex configuration.

Design for reuse

Configurability allowed a solution originating from one customer requirement to support Sales, Customer Service and Marketing use cases.

Let evidence guide iteration

Adoption and customer feedback provide a stronger basis for prioritizing advanced capabilities than assumptions about what customers might need.

PM takeaway Start with the simplest solution that solves the validated problem, measure whether customers adopt it, and let evidence—not assumptions—determine where to add complexity.
